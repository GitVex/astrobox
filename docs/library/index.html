Cross-frames hold systems, brackets mark attention, orbits give an entity its place in the sky, hairline cells hold peers. Orbits are rationed: the rules below say where they may appear and how apps talk to them.

Dashes that run past the card edge

Each card clips an ellipse larger than itself, so the orbit seems to continue outside. The card's text is hashed into a seed that sets the orbit's entry edge, size, tilt, second ring and satellite. The same text always draws the same orbit.

Orbit rulesFRM—11

Where orbits may appear, and how many

AllowedEntity cards with an identity: worlds, factions, ships, people. The one featured object on a screen, such as a bracket plate, an image plate or the hero. Pull quotes on reading pages.
NeverTables, forms, controls, navigation, toasts, lists and thumbnails. Nothing inside a cross-frame table: construction lines and orbits never share a surface.
BudgetOne visible orbit field per viewport: a featured orbit or one row of entity cards, never both. At most two rings and one satellite per element.
Getting around the budgetDefine orbits on as many elements as you like, but reveal them on hover or focus with data-orbit-reveal="hover", so only one ever shows. Touch screens light the card nearest the middle of the screen.
Mechanical screensScreens with data-voice="mechanical" hide orbits, unless an element opts in with data-orbit-optin. See Voices in Foundations.
Motion and meaningDrift only, 40 to 90 seconds a loop and slower over images, with no hover-driven animation. Reduced motion stops it. Orbits are hidden from screen readers; mood is always also stated in words.

KindsGLY—14

Every record gets a mark of its own

A glyph belongs to a record, not to a word: it appears only where the markup carries an entity's ID from the database, whatever its kind. Kind sets the core shape, the entity's ID seeds the orbit and satellite position, and data-mood colours the orbit and satellite while the core stays ink, so the kind always reads. The glyph shares its tilt with the entity's card orbit, and renaming an entity keeps its glyph.

Lexicon · Worlds · Vey Reach

The drowned archive of Aster Vey

Twice a day the tide climbs into the lower stacks, and twice a day the archivists carry what they can upstairs.

01Arrival

The archive was built when the sea sat three kilometres further out. Its founders cut the reading halls into the cliff and sealed the record vaults below the waterline, trusting pumps that have not run since the Scream.1 What survives today survives because someone chose to carry it.

The crew came in on the evening tide, after six jumps through the Corvane drill. From orbit the world looked like any other ocean planet. From the landing pad it looked like a library that had lost an argument with the sea.

02The tidal stacks

The stacks are terraces cut into the cliff, one above the other, each holding the records of a single century. The oldest sit lowest. That was a sensible choice until the water rose.

Twice a day the lower three terraces flood to the ceiling. The archivists know the tide tables by heart and work in shifts: one crew clears the shelves as the water comes in, another dries and sorts what was saved. The lit windows you can see from the water at dusk belong to the sorters.

03The Ledger

The current keeper, , will trade access for salvage. Her people work the upper galleries by lamplight and answer only to her.

The Ledger does not sell records. It trades them one for one, and it keeps a list of every debt it is owed. Archivists say the list of debts is longer than the archive itself.

04What the crew owes

The keeper asks for one thing she will not name in front of offworlders: a drive core marked Q7Ʒ-VΞX.2 In return she has promised the crew a single record from the sealed vaults, chosen by them.

Nobody has told her that the sealed vaults have been underwater for forty years. She may already know.

Tooltip ruleUNI—49

Only when it adds something

Tooltip ruleA value gets a tooltip only when it adds information the reader can't already see: a real-world conversion, the source of an estimate, a roll's average and range, the real date of an in-world time, or the baseline of a change. Never restate what the value already says. If there is nothing to add, leave the tooltip out. Values with a tooltip show a faint underline on hover.

States

Empty · loading · error · access

Every component keeps its shape in every state, so nothing jumps when data arrives, fails or is withheld.

State rulesSTA—51

Five states, one shape

EmptyNothing yetThe frame stays. One sentence names what will appear, and one action adds the first item. Orbit cards keep the orbit but lose the satellite.“No fittings logged yet.”
LoadingAfter 300 msHatched blocks in the shape of the coming content. Shown only after 300 ms, so fast loads never flicker, and swapped out without moving anything.No text, no spinner.
ErrorRose edgeWhat went wrong in one sentence, how to fix it in another, and a Retry button. No apology, no error codes up front.“Couldn't reach the Datapad server.”
StaleDashedWhen a refresh fails but older data exists, the data stays and turns provisional: dashed lines and the time of the last good sync.“Last synced 19:42.”
No accessBarsShows that something exists and how long it is, never what it says. The server sends word lengths; each word becomes a bar and the spaces stay real, so withheld text wraps like text. One line gives the reason.“Revealed in session.”

Motion rulesMOT—58

What may move, and what never does

May moveFrames drawing in once, indicators settling, content swapping in place, live values counting, orbits drifting.
Never movesLayout around the reader, text being read, anything on hover except a tooltip, parallax, and any bounce other than the plus marks.
LoopsOnly orbit drift and the loading shimmer loop. Both stop offscreen and under reduced motion.
WaitingNothing appears for the first 300 ms of a load. Fast answers arrive without any loading state at all.
Reduced motionMOT—59

Movement stops, fades stay

draw, popFrames and plus marks appear at once
settleIndicators and brackets jump to place
swapKept as a short fade
countValues change straight to the new number
driftOrbits hold still; the plate's satellites stop
pressNo scale; colour change only

Opacity and colour fades don't cause motion sickness, so they remain the one way to show change when movement is off.

InstallEXP—63

    
Three files and one call

    
<!-- plain HTML -->
<link rel="stylesheet" href="astrobox.tokens.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="astrobox.css">
<script src="astrobox.js"></script>

// Nuxt: the layer's plugin inits after hydration
export default defineNuxtConfig({ extends: ['astrobox'] })

// after rendering new markup
Astrobox.init(container)
